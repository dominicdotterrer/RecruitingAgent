# Statement of Ambition

## Who I Am

I am a mathematician by training and a geometer by nature. My PhD work on isoperimetric problems in random polyhedra was not a detour from applied work — it was the formation of a way of thinking. I look at the world in terms of structure, shape, boundary, and constraint. That lens has served me well across plasma physics, financial markets, differential privacy, and agentic AI systems. The common thread is not the domain — it is the geometry of the problem.

I am at my best when a problem has no established solution, when the first task is to figure out what the problem actually *is*, and when the answer requires building something new. I am not looking for a role where the path is already paved. I am looking for a role where my job is to find the path.

---

## What I Am Looking For

### Continuous and Deep Learning

My primary objective in any role is to keep learning. Not breadth for its own sake, but the opportunity to go deep into a new domain, develop genuine fluency in it, and bring mathematical clarity to problems that practitioners have been approaching with intuition. I will prioritize a role that exposes me to a new field I don't yet know well over a role that asks me to do more of what I have already done, even if the latter pays better or carries a more impressive title.

### Physical and Geometric Systems

The class of problems I find most compelling involves physical systems — systems where the domain has intrinsic geometry, where space and time and constraint are not abstractions but real operative forces.

This includes but is not limited to:
- **Autonomous systems and robotics**: drone swarm control, path planning, sensor fusion, edge inference under physical latency constraints
- **Geospatial and network problems**: traffic flow design, telecommunications network topology, GIS applications, logistics optimization
- **Manufacturing and fabrication**: semiconductor process modeling, CNC tool path optimization, quality control on physical production lines, any domain where a physical process produces a signal that can be modeled and controlled
- **Environmental and earth systems**: weather modeling, climate data, hydrology, ecological modeling — any system where the geometry of the physical world is a primary variable
- **Defense technology**: autonomous platforms, ISR systems, battlespace understanding — particularly where the challenge is real-time inference under adversarial conditions and hard resource constraints

I interpret "physical systems" broadly. The criterion is: does the geometry of the real world impose non-trivial constraints on the problem? If the answer is yes, I am interested. A tree surgeon mapping canopy coverage with LiDAR qualifies. A generic SaaS analytics dashboard does not.

**One important exception**: financial markets. They are not physical, but they are mathematically deep in ways I respect — stochastic processes, information theory, microstructure, and latent state estimation all apply. I have done substantive work in this domain and remain open to roles where the mathematical challenge is genuine, particularly in research or quantitative R&D contexts.

**What does not qualify**: pure enterprise software, administrative data science, claims processing, healthcare operations analytics, or any role where the primary output is a report or dashboard rather than a model or a system. I have done this work and I know it is not where I do my best thinking.

## How I Want to Work

### Teaching and Mentorship

Teaching is not something I do on the side — it is central to how I think and how I lead. I believe that most technical teams underperform not because they lack tools, but because they lack the mathematical intuition to know when a tool's assumptions are broken. My most valuable contribution to a team is often helping people formulate the problem correctly before they start building.

I am looking for a role where this kind of intellectual leadership is valued. That can take many forms: formal mentorship of junior scientists, customer-facing technical scoping, internal R&D problem formulation, or cross-functional translation between research and engineering. I am equally comfortable as an IC, a team lead, or a manager of managers. What matters is that the role gives me the opportunity to bring clarity of thought to people who are trying to solve hard problems.

### Thought Leadership

I want to work for an organization that has a clear intellectual philosophy and is trying to execute on it. That means an organization that values research output — publications, conference proceedings, patents, white papers, or substantive technical blog posts — not just shipped products. I am not looking for a company that tolerates research as a recruiting tool. I am looking for a company where research is how it competes.

## Location and Logistics

I want to be around people. I work better in environments where I can walk to a whiteboard with a colleague, have an unplanned conversation that changes how I think about a problem, or mentor someone in person. I am not interested in fully remote roles.

**Preferred locations**: Maryland, Washington DC, and Northern Virginia (the DMV corridor). I am currently in the Ashburn, VA area and am familiar with the defense technology and federal contractor ecosystem in the region.

I am open to relocating for an exceptional opportunity — particularly to a hub with a strong physical AI, robotics, or defense tech concentration (Boston, Pittsburgh, Austin, San Diego, and the Bay Area would all be worth considering for the right role).

**Security clearance**: I do not currently hold a TS/SCI clearance but I am a US citizen and am eligible to be cleared. Roles that require an active clearance on day one are out of scope. Roles that sponsor clearance are in scope.

---

## Career Progression

Whatever I do next must represent clear forward movement — either into a domain I am not yet expert in (upskilling), into a larger organizational mandate (greater scope and seniority), or both. I am not interested in a lateral move that offers more salary for the same intellectual territory. The question I will ask of any role is: will I be demonstrably more capable, more senior, or more knowledgeable in two years than I am today because of this role?

---

## Scoring Guidance for the Agent

When evaluating roles against this profile, apply the following priorities in order:

1. **Depth of learning opportunity** — does this role expose genuine new intellectual territory?
2. **Physical / geometric domain** — does the problem domain involve real-world physical systems, geospatial reasoning, or mathematical market structure?
3. **Research and thought leadership** — does the organization produce research output and value intellectual leadership?
4. **Teaching / mentorship opportunity** — is there a team to lead, mentor, or communicate with technically?
5. **Location fit** — DMV-region preferred; relocation considered for strong matches on criteria 1–4
6. **Clearance** — sponsorship acceptable; active requirement on day one is disqualifying

**Hard filters** (a role failing any of these should score no higher than 3/10):
- Fully remote with no in-person component
- Requires active TS/SCI clearance on day one
- Primary domain is enterprise SaaS, healthcare operations, or administrative analytics
- Role is clearly junior (IC with no mentorship or leadership component)

**Important**: always surface the best available match, even if no role scores above 6. If the digest would otherwise be empty, include the top 3 scoring roles regardless of threshold, flagged clearly as "best available — below threshold."
